# Timely Filing Limits by Payer

Timely filing is the deadline by which a claim must reach the payer after the date of service. Missing it means the claim is denied as unrecoverable — you cannot bill the patient for timely-filing denials.

## Commercial Payers

### Aetna — 120 days
Claims must be received within 120 days of the date of service. Corrected claims follow the same 120-day window. Appeals must be filed within 180 days of the denial date.

### Cigna — 180 days
Claims must be received within 180 days of the date of service. Appeals within 180 days of the initial determination.

### UnitedHealthcare (UHC) — 90 days
One of the tightest windows in commercial insurance. Claims must be received within 90 days of the date of service. This is the payer most likely to generate timely-filing denials — prioritize UHC claims in your daily worklist. Appeals within 180 days.

### Anthem BCBS — 180 days
Claims must be received within 180 days of the date of service. Note: individual BCBS plans (Empire, etc.) may have their own variations — always verify against the member's specific plan.

### Humana — 180 days
Claims must be received within 180 days of the date of service.

## Government Payers

### Medicare (Traditional) — 365 days
Claims must be received no later than 1 calendar year from the date of service. This is set by federal regulation and is the most generous window, but do not let it breed complacency — file Medicare claims within 30 days as a best practice.

### Medicaid NY — 90 days
New York Medicaid requires claims within 90 days of the date of service. Extremely tight for a government payer. Prioritize NY Medicaid claims immediately — they expire faster than almost any other payer.

## Key Rules

1. **Timely-filing denials cannot be billed to the patient.** The loss falls entirely on the provider. This is why filing deadlines are the highest-priority work in any billing operation.
2. **The clock starts on the date of service**, not the date you discovered the claim.
3. **Corrected claims** generally must also fall within the original timely-filing window.
4. **Proof of timely filing**: always keep electronic submission confirmations (999/277CA acknowledgments). If a payer claims non-receipt, the acknowledgment is your evidence.
5. **Weekends and holidays count** — these are calendar days, not business days.
